Feedback That Lands

Giving specific, useful feedback, and taking it well

Why this course. New leaders often avoid feedback or keep it vague to be kind. But “great job” teaches nothing and “be more of a team player” starts an argument. Here you'll practise the alternative.

“Great job today!”
On the 11am call, you repeated Mr Patel's concern back before offering options. He emailed to thank us. Could you share how at Friday's huddle?

🗺️How this works

  1. Part 1, fix it: pick the strongest version of four vague comments.
  2. Part 2, build it: assemble feedback piece by piece, from situation to next step, and watch it come together.
  3. Part 3, receive it: choose how to respond when the feedback is about you.
  4. See your feedback style, XP, rank and badges at the end.

Tip. Watch for words that describe the person rather than what they did. Press A, B or C to choose in parts 1 and 3.

The feedback formula

  1. Situation. When and where, so it's anchored in a real moment.
  2. Behaviour. What you saw or heard, not a label about the person.
  3. Impact. The effect it had on customers, the team or results.
  4. Next step. A question or request that opens a conversation.

Use it for praise as much as for problems. Specific praise tells people exactly what to repeat.
